# Base Material
  FR-4 is the most popular substrate material for rigid PCBs.
  Flex PCBs utilize a thin, flexible polymer film as the substrate for the circuitry. Flex PCBs reduce space consumption, and have lower weight.
  Aluminum/copper core boards have better heat dissipation and thermal transfer than standard FR-4 constructions.
  *Note: For Aluminum/Copper core boards, the min drill size is 1.0mm, and min slot width is 1.6mm.*
  Rogers/Teflon PCBs have excellent dielectric properties and minimal resistance. They are generally used in high-speed, high-frequency circuitry applications.
  [Learn More](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/jlcpcb-base-material)
  ## Options
  - **FR-4**
  - **Flex**: Good flexibility, reduced space and weight, higher density, minimized errors.
  - **Aluminum**: Good thermal conductivity and electrical insulation properties, LED applications.
  - **Copper Core**: High thermal conductivity and heat dissipation, high-power supply and LED applications.
  - **Rogers**: Low dissipation factor, solid dimensional stability, high-frequency applications.
  - **PTFE Teflon**: Lower dissipation factor, high temperature and high frequency applications.
  - **HDI**: Microvia tech with minimum laser via size 75μm; blind via plating and filling with dimple ≤ 10μm; maximum aspect ratio 1:1.

# Via Covering
  The production is based on your Gerber files. If you choose Epoxy/Copper paste Filled & Capped, only untented vias ≤ 0.3mm will be filled with epoxy resin. For tented vias, only those ≤ 0.5mm will be filled.
  Vias that need filling should be marked in your remarks when:
  1. Untented vias between 0.3mm and 0.5mm need to be filled.
  2. Untented vias ≤ 0.3mm don't need to be filled.
  3. Tented vias ≤ 0.5mm don't need to be filled.
  4. Specific via sizes or areas need to be filled or left unfilled.
  5. Your Gerber includes a via plugging/filling layer.
  Pads corresponding to the vias will be made untented based on your design. It's recommended to leave a remark indicating the specific via sizes or areas that need filling, or to note when your Gerber includes a via plugging/filling layer. [Click here to learn more about via covering](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/pcb-via-covering).
  ## Options
  - **Tented:** Vias are covered with soldermask and are not processed with HASL or ENIG. Boards with 6+ layers are upgraded to Epoxy Filled & Capped for free.
  - **Untented:** Soldermask is removed over the via and its annular ring. The via is solderable and has the same surface finish as regular pads.
  - **Plugged:** Filled with solder resist, at least 70% coverage. Low cost and simple process, but the surface is uneven. Suited to non-BGA boards that tolerate poor evenness.
  - **Epoxy Filled & Capped:** Filled with epoxy resin and plated over for a flat, solderable surface. Ideal for via-in-pad, multilayer, and thick boards. Vias should be at most 0.5 mm in diameter. Boards with 6+ layers are upgraded to this option for free.
  - **Copper Paste Filled & Capped** (currently disabled): Filled with high thermal conductivity copper paste (8 W/m·K) and plated over. Ideal for via-in-pad and heatsink pads. Vias should be at most 0.5 mm in diameter.
  Per the table, Epoxy/Copper paste Filled & Capped has at least 95% coverage.
# Via Plating Method
  Horizontal Electroless Copper Plating is a chemical deposition process that forms a thin, uniform copper layer on the non-conductive walls of drilled holes in a PCB.
  Conductive Adhesive coats the via walls with a conductive polymer adhesive containing metallic filler particles. The adhesive adsorbs copper ions to deposit a copper layer.

# Board Outline Tolerance
  Regular and precision CNC routing differ mainly in how the PCB is aligned and the number of routing passes. Regular routing fastens the panel at points outside individual PCBs and is a single-pass process. Precision routing uses alignment holes within each board for positioning and includes a rough pass followed by a fine pass.
  ## Options
  - **±0.2mm (Regular)**
  - **±0.1mm (Precision)**
  ## Precision Routing Requirements
  1. The PCB must contain alignment holes (⌀ 1.5 mm minimum, circular, preferably non-plated).
  2. At least 3 alignment holes are required.
  3. Alignment holes should be placed across the PCB and not clustered together.
  4. Individually routed boards need to be at least 50×50 mm for precision routing.
  5. Boards in panels only need to satisfy the alignment hole diameter requirement to use precision routing.
  **Example:** Alignment holes at both ends of the board allow good positioning (OK). Alignment holes off to one side of the board give inaccurate positioning (NG).

# Board Cleaning
  JLCPCB uses no-clean solder paste, and SMD solder joints are not cleaned by default. After wave soldering, a small amount of transparent, slightly tacky flux residue may remain on the board. It does not affect functionality.
  For boards with through-hole components and strict appearance requirements, JLCPCB offers two cleaning services, dry-ice cleaning and solvent-based cleaning. Both remove flux residue, solder balls, fingerprints, dust, and other contaminants. Dry-ice cleaning costs about $0.16 per piece, and solvent-based cleaning costs about $0.25 per piece. Both add 1 day to the lead time.
  For orders with sensitive components such as temperature sensors, microphones, crystal oscillators, or cameras, select "Caution for Special Parts" and list the designators or part numbers in the remarks.

# Nitrogen Reflow Soldering
  Nitrogen reflow soldering is based on hot-air reflow. Nitrogen is added during heating to lower the oxygen level in the oven. This prevents component pin oxidation, improves solder wetting, reduces bubbles in solder joints, and improves reliability. It extends the lead time by 1–2 days. [Learn more](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/reflow-soldering-principles-and-precautions)
  ## Options
  - **No** (disabled; not available for Economic PCB Assembly)
  - **Yes (for Standard)** (disabled; not available for Economic PCB Assembly)
  - **Yes (for Economic)** (selected; this is the only option shown as active for Economic PCB Assembly)
# Function Test
  **FCT (Functional Circuit Test)** tests the function of assembled PCBA products with a power-on test, to determine whether they meet design requirements. If needed, customers provide a specific test method, and JLCPCB evaluates whether it is feasible.
  The extra cost includes a $15.70 engineering fee and a $7.86/hour labor fee. Delivery time is extended depending on the difficulty and the number of boards.
  Burning firmware can also serve as a functional test. To evaluate this, JLCPCB needs:
  - The manufacturer part number of the chip
  - The programming interface definition reserved on the board
  - A picture of the board
  - The program file in HEX or BIN format
  ## Options
  - **No** (selected by default)
  - Yes (disabled; not available for Economic PCB Assembly)
